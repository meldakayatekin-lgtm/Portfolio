About

01 — background

At Stony Brook, I lead the VIP BEAR household assistive technology team. We work with United Cerebral Palsy (UCP) to turn everyday tasks, like reaching an item at the back of a counter, into design and testing questions.

I also work in the engineering makerspace and as a Quality Engineering Intern at Natech Plastics. Moving between printing prototypes and inspecting molded parts has made me pay closer attention to fit, tolerances, and what happens when a mechanism carries a load.

Motorized Toddler Mobility Trainer

Our team adapted a manual trainer for children with limited upper-body strength. A joystick controls a motor-driven roller that turns the existing wheel. Under load, traction at the roller–wheel interface limited performance, so we used contact force and motor torque to guide the mount design.

Microfluidic biosensor concept for Alzheimer’s biomarkers

For BME 100, I designed a concept for an implantable glass chip that would sample cerebrospinal fluid. The proposed channels route fluid to electrochemical sensors for Aβ40, Aβ42, and phosphorylated tau. The design received a faculty honorable mention.

3D-printed Braille keyboard

A 3D printed Braille keyboard designed from scratch in AutoCAD, with custom keycaps embossed in Braille for tactile typing. The project is in progress, currently focused on integrating a voice sensor channel that will audibly announce each key as it is typed, adding a real time feedback layer for users who are blind or visually impaired.
